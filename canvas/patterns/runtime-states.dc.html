Runtime action states

The nine states every state-changing surface handles.

sprint 055 · phase D.1 · §6 of the design philosophy · Nielsen (1993) latency thresholds

A tap on a state-changing button walks the caller through some of these states. The 0.1 s / 1 s / 10 s thresholds are Nielsen's — feels instantaneous, holds the user's flow, still worth waiting for. Anything longer becomes a background job with its own state. Every state carries factual copy; no reassurance, no narration.

Retry
The driver call did not return. The UI does not claim success or failure until it does. Retry is offered only when the operation is idempotency-safe; otherwise the UI reloads and re-reads.

Longer than 10 s: the action becomes a background job with its own progress indicator and its own return path. The close pipeline is an example — AttemptRunClose triggers four system-worker stages, and the close console renders each stage as it walks.
